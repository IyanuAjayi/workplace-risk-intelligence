# Design benchmark: five reference products

Reviewed 31 Oct 2026 for the Workplace Risk Intelligence report. One researcher per reference opened the pages, the linked dashboards and the screenshots, and recorded only what they actually saw. The aim was transferable design lessons, not content.

## What each reference turned out to be

| Reference | What it is | Real Power BI? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [tudzai Power BI Decision Products](https://tudzai.github.io/Portfolio/showcase/powerbi/index.html) | A job-seeking portfolio of 20 finance dashboards with synthetic data | No. The previews are hand-built HTML and SVG in Segoe UI. No .pbix is published |
| [ZoomCharts finance examples](https://www.zoomcharts.com/en/microsoft-power-bi-custom-visuals/dashboard-and-report-examples/power-bi-finance-dashboard-templates) | A vendor gallery of community templates built to sell paid custom visuals | Yes. Two publish-to-web reports opened and responded to slicers, but one broke ("--", -100.00%) when 2024 was selected |
| [ABN AMRO customer story](https://www.microsoft.com/en/customers/story/22167-abn-amro-bank-power-bi) | Partner marketing for Nova Silva custom visuals. The "risk" is project delivery risk | No report shown. Claims (70%, 50%, 20%) have no baseline or method |
| [Apollop24 Enterprise Financial Portfolio](https://github.com/Apollop24/EnterpriseFinancialPortfolio) | A one-person GitHub showcase of 12 finance pages | A .pbix exists but was not opened. The data is generated by the repo's own random-number script despite claiming official sources |
| [Microsoft Power BI samples](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-gb/power-bi/create-reports/sample-datasets) | Microsoft documentation for eight refreshed sample reports | Yes, as downloadable .pbix files. The screenshots are curated stills |

None of the five is an insurance or workplace-safety product, and only two show working Power BI. Their value is in specific patterns, and in the mistakes they make that an underwriting audience would not forgive.

## Lessons we adopt

1. **Lead each page with its question and a one-sentence finding.** Every reference titles charts with what is plotted ("EAD by Rating"), not what it means. The best moment in the set is a ZoomCharts regional page that ends on one bold takeaway. Each of our pages opens with a finding and its caveat.
2. **Give every KPI its context.** Value, period, the comparison it is judged against, and the gap, in words as well as colour (the Le Merrer "VS Budget" card, Microsoft's Corporate Spend cards). For us: a band's crossing rate against the base rate, a model's AUC against the rating-class baseline.
3. **One restrained palette, with colour reserved for meaning.** The weakest references change hue on every page or give every KPI its own colour, so colour says nothing. The ZoomCharts Debtor report keeps one alert colour for one meaning everywhere. We keep the brand accent separate from the ordered risk-band colours, and pair colour with a second cue (label, position or shape).
4. **Provenance on every page.** No refreshed Microsoft sample shows a source, as-of date or caveat. Ours carries the source, coverage window, extract date, test years and "Simulated" where it applies, on the page itself.
5. **Definitions inside the report.** The ZoomCharts Financial Analysis template has a glossary page with formulas, and tudzai case pages write formulas out in plain words. We define the target, DART rate, AUC and the pricing scenarios where they are used, with a methods section.
6. **Show the arithmetic and the base.** Print how a figure is built (simulated premium = industry rate x hours x claim cost) and the sample size behind any comparison. Tooltips written as sentences (Microsoft samples) carry the count and period.
7. **Scenarios side by side, always labelled.** tudzai's hub headlines a non-default scenario without naming it. Our claim-cost scenario stays named on every pricing figure, and Scenario A and B stay visible together.
8. **One layout learned once.** A fixed header, navigation band and KPI strip in the same order on every page, with natural sort orders (risk band 1 to 5, size bands ascending), one obvious entry page and a visible reset.
9. **Native visuals first.** ZoomCharts and ABN AMRO depend on licensed custom visuals at EUR 1,860 to 2,820 a year per capacity. Everything we need is native Power BI over a designed background.
10. **Test every state, not the screenshot.** The ZoomCharts report and the tudzai previews break or contradict themselves under some filters. We test each slicer state against `outputs/powerbi/expected_values.csv`.

## What we avoid

- Claims that cannot be traced to a query, and any figure that disagrees with another figure on the same page.
- Power BI defaults left in place: auto-generated titles, raw field names, cut-off or rotated labels, unrounded decimals, rates shown as 0.30, alphabetical months or bands.
- Decoration that costs accuracy: gradients, drop shadows, donuts for two or three categories, maps where a ranked bar reads more precisely, "neon on dark" showcase styling.
- Associations presented as drivers. The Key Influencers style "what drives" language needs the caveat and the sample size; our drivers chart is labelled as associations, not causes.
- Presenting a web mock-up as a working report. Our concept boards carry a corner label saying they are design concepts, not live Power BI.
